# Forecast postprocessing: model and input options

Research note for the planned UK IFS-to-ERA5 experiment. This proposes a test, not a result: the matched dataset and correction model are not yet validated.

## What published examples suggest

- A [solar irradiance NWP postprocessing benchmark](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X22001839) compared statistical learning methods for day-ahead irradiance. Its reported ordering varied with the loss metric: more complex methods including gradient boosting generally had lower mean squared error, while support vector regression performed better on mean absolute error. This is a reason to compare methods and metrics, not to assume those rankings transfer to the UK or ERA5.
- [Phipps et al.](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000145553/149863404) compared linear regression and random forests in a wind-power ensemble forecasting study. Their inputs included wind speed and components, pressure, temperature and seasonal indicators; models were fitted by horizon. Their target was power and their training setup used historical weather, so it is an example of model and feature choices, not proof of skill for 100 m wind-speed correction.
- A [wind-gust ensemble postprocessing comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09512) found that additional meteorological predictors improved skill in its study. Gusts, surface observations and probabilistic ensembles differ from this project's deterministic 100 m wind speed and ERA5 reference.

## A modest first experiment

For each matched location and valid hour, fit separate wind and solar models to `error = ERA5 − IFS`; predict `corrected = IFS + predicted error`. Retain raw IFS as the essential baseline, then compare a training-set mean bias correction, linear or ridge regression, and a constrained-complexity gradient-boosted tree or random forest. Tune only on validation data. The residual formulation is a design choice; direct prediction of ERA5 is also possible. Enforce nonnegative corrected speed and radiation if used, and evaluate the same postprocessing rule on validation and test data.

Begin with variables available from the **same forecast run** at prediction time: forecast value, lead hour, valid-time hour and annual cycle, and location coordinates or stable land/sea metadata. Encode cyclic time using sine/cosine. Candidate additional inputs, subject to actual availability and a fresh data audit, include forecast wind direction encoded cyclically, 10 m wind, temperature, pressure and cloud cover; for solar consider solar geometry or top-of-atmosphere radiation and forecast cloud cover. Compare a small feature set with additions on held-out data; do not use same-valid-time ERA5 or future observations as predictors. Use historical reference values only if their real operational availability and latency are established at the forecast decision time.

Use a chronological train/validation/test split by run date across all locations, with preprocessing fitted on training only. Report MAE, RMSE and signed bias against ERA5 for raw and corrected forecasts, by lead, location, season and land/sea. For solar show all hours and daylight separately, with the daylight definition fixed in advance; nighttime zeros can otherwise dominate. Test generalisation to held-out locations separately if it matters. Keep the final test untouched during model selection and account for correlated hours and locations when assessing uncertainty.

## Timing and data limitations

- Define the decision time and target hours first. The previous day's 12 UTC initialization is not necessarily the time the run became available; verify publication latency before claiming an operational day-ahead backtest. Store run initialization, valid time and lead for every row. [Open-Meteo's Single Runs API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/single-runs-api) selects a run explicitly. Its documentation identifies a historical IFS Cycle 49R1 hindcast archive from March 2024 and a later Cycle 50R1 change: do not assume every record is an as-issued historical forecast, and assess effects of model-cycle changes.
- Open-Meteo describes hourly `shortwave_radiation` as a **preceding-hour mean** in W/m², whereas wind speed is instantaneous; verify both endpoints' definitions and match the same intervals, units, locations and cell-selection methods. [Open-Meteo forecast documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) explains these conventions and land, sea and nearest grid-cell selection. Audit grid coordinates and missing values before fitting.
- ERA5 is a reanalysis reference, not independent station truth. Better agreement with ERA5 does not establish improved real-world wind or irradiance forecasts, and neither weather variable is electricity generation. Validate against independent measurements before drawing operational conclusions. Historical coverage, run provenance and variable consistency remain prerequisites, not established facts.
